Welcome to My Project

Communication is an essential part of healthcare. As a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA), I use different types of language when communicating with nurses, other CNAs, and patients.

This project explores how medical terminology, electronic documentation, and patient-friendly language support effective communication and patient safety in the hospital community.

My Central Argument

In healthcare, effective communication requires more than knowing medical terminology; CNAs and nurses must adapt their language to different audiences, document patient information accurately, and use clear communication strategies to support teamwork, patient understanding, and safety.

Professional Communication in Healthcare

Medical Terminology and Teamwork

As a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA), I communicate with nurses and other CNAs throughout my shifts. When discussing patient care with coworkers, I use professional language and appropriate medical terminology to communicate clearly and efficiently.

For example, healthcare workers commonly use abbreviations such as BP for blood pressure. When an urgent measurement is needed, a nurse might request STAT vital signs. The word STAT communicates that the task requires immediate attention.

According to The Joint Commission (2026), healthcare organizations must follow requirements for using abbreviations, acronyms, and symbols in clinical documentation. Certain abbreviations are prohibited in specific types of documentation because they may cause confusion or errors.

This research helps me understand that professional communication is not only about using medical terminology quickly. It is also about choosing words and abbreviations carefully. As a CNA, I must communicate clearly with nurses and other healthcare professionals to support accurate information sharing and patient safety.

Adapting Language to My Audience

I have learned that communicating with healthcare professionals requires different language choices than communicating with patients. When speaking with nurses or other CNAs, I use precise medical terms to share important information.

Clear communication also involves confirming important instructions. In my workplace, nurses and CNAs frequently repeat or confirm information to help prevent misunderstandings and support patient safety.

According to the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (2023), check-back communication helps healthcare professionals confirm that important information has been received and understood correctly.

This research connects to my experience as a CNA because nurses and CNAs at my hospital frequently repeat or confirm important instructions. This practice shows how professional communication supports teamwork and helps reduce misunderstandings during patient care.

Why These Language Choices Matter

These communication practices reflect the shared values of the healthcare community, including accuracy, efficiency, teamwork, and patient safety. Medical terminology helps healthcare professionals communicate specific information, while confirming instructions helps ensure that important messages are understood correctly.

Electronic Documentation in Patient Care

Writing in Electronic Health Records

As a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA), electronic documentation is an important part of my daily responsibilities. I use the hospital's electronic health record (EHR) system to document patient care, including vital signs, daily activities, food and fluid intake, weight, and other observations.

I record vital signs at intervals based on the patient's condition and care instructions. When I take blood pressure manually, I enter the measurement into the electronic chart. When I use a vital signs machine that automatically transfers readings, I check the chart to confirm that the information was recorded correctly.

Accuracy and Responsibility

Sometimes, information from the vital signs machine does not transfer successfully. When this happens, I enter the information manually and verify the documentation. I also document other care activities, such as showers, daily hygiene, meals, fluid intake, and patient weight.

When necessary, I add comments to describe relevant observations. These comments need to be clear, objective, and professional because other healthcare team members may rely on the information.

Documentation as a Literacy Practice

Electronic charting is a form of professional writing that follows the expectations of the healthcare community. Unlike informal writing, clinical documentation requires accurate information, appropriate terminology, and attention to detail. These writing practices help healthcare workers communicate patient information and support continuity of care.

According to the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (2025), electronic health records help healthcare professionals access and share patient information to support coordinated care.

This research connects to my daily responsibilities as a CNA because I use electronic health records to document vital signs, patient weight, intake and output, and other care activities. I also verify that measurements from electronic devices have been transferred correctly into the patient's chart. These practices show that clinical writing requires accuracy, attention to detail, and responsibility. Clear documentation helps other healthcare professionals understand the care provided and supports continuity of patient care.

Patient Communication and Health Literacy

Using Plain Language with Patients

As a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA), I understand that communicating with patients requires a different approach than communicating with nurses and other healthcare professionals. When speaking with patients, I try to use simple and familiar words instead of complicated medical terminology.

For example, when communicating with coworkers, I might use the abbreviation BP. However, when speaking with patients, I usually say blood pressure because it is easier to understand. I also explain what I am going to do before providing care.

Explaining and Demonstrating Procedures

Sometimes patients do not understand an explanation the first time. When this happens, I use different, simpler words or demonstrate what I am explaining whenever possible.

For example, when taking a patient's blood pressure, I sometimes notice that their legs are crossed or that they are moving their arm during the measurement. I explain that these movements or positions can affect the accuracy of the reading.

I ask patients to keep their feet flat on the floor, relax their arm, and remain still while I take their blood pressure. If necessary, I demonstrate the correct position so they can understand what I need them to do.

The Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (2024) recommends using plain language, avoiding complicated medical terminology, and using visual explanations or demonstrations to help patients understand healthcare information.

These recommendations reflect my experience as a CNA. When patients need help understanding a procedure, I adjust my explanations and demonstrate what they should do. For example, during blood pressure measurements, I explain the importance of keeping their arm relaxed and remaining still. This shows how adapting language and using demonstrations can make healthcare communication more accessible and understandable.

Why Patient-Centered Communication Matters

These experiences have taught me that effective communication is not only about providing information. It is also about making sure that the information is understandable to the person receiving it.

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2024), health literacy involves the ability to find, understand, and use health information to make informed decisions. Healthcare organizations also have a responsibility to make health information easier for people to understand.

This research connects to my experience as a CNA because I adapt my language when explaining procedures to patients. For example, when taking blood pressure, I use simple instructions and demonstrations to explain the correct position. These communication choices help patients understand what is happening and participate more confidently in their care.

By adapting my vocabulary and using demonstrations, I can help patients feel more comfortable, informed, and involved in their care. These communication choices reflect the importance of patience, respect, and understanding in healthcare.
